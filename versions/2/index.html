Work · Case study

Yum Tales Supply.

Food supply, Athens. Twelve months of the unglamorous work: fewer promotions, better measurement, and a second order that finally arrived on its own.

The problem

Sales looked healthy and the ad account looked healthy. The customer file did not. Four in five buyers never came back, and every good month needed a discount to make it good.

What we did

Rebuilt measurement around cohorts instead of campaigns. Cut the two campaigns that bought one-time discount hunters. Rewrote the post-purchase flow around reorder timing, and rebuilt three product pages so a returning customer could reorder in two taps.

What we did not do

We did not raise the budget for seven months. Twice we recommended spending less. The monthly report said so in one line, at the top.

Services

What we do, and how we report it.

Three services that share one measurement model. You get the same four numbers every month, in the same order, whether they flatter us or not.

01

Performance

Meta and Google, bought against repeat purchase. We split new from returning revenue at the account level, so a good month cannot hide behind a discount. Creative is tested in small, boring increments.

How we report it New customers, cost per new customer, returning revenue share, and contribution after ad spend. Four numbers, one page, every month.

02

Web

Sites and shops in Shopify or headless, built so the second order takes two taps. Speed, clarity, and a checkout that does not lose people. Measurement is wired in before launch, not after.

How we report it Conversion by customer type, reorder rate, page speed on real devices, and what changed since the last release.

03

Social

The channel between orders. Content your existing customers recognise, in a voice that sounds like the business rather than a trend. What performs organically becomes paid, not the other way round.

How we report it Reach we paid for versus reach we earned, saves and replies, and how much returning revenue arrived through the channel.

What a month looks like

  1. Week 1

    A short call, a one-page report, and the number we got wrong last month.

  2. Weeks 2 & 3

    The work. Tests run small, changes ship, nothing is announced before it is measured.

  3. Week 4

    Cohorts close. We write the plain-numbers page for the month, including what we would hold or cut.
